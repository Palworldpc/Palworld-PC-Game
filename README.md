The protagonist's biography <a href="https://store.steampowered.com/app/1623730/Palworld/">presents</a> a typical adventurer seeking to uncover all the hidden corners of the world. Encountering a wonderful semblance of a protective barrier along the way, the protagonist suffers shipwreck. The waves wash the unfortunate explorer onto an archipelago that is not marked on any map. A true "terra incognita" with traces of artificial origin — mysterious creatures, artifacts, portals to other dimensions, ruins of the Ancients, and a giant world tree in the center.

The plot and narrative were not the focus of Palworld's early access. There are a couple of characters to chat with, as well as story bosses and factions. The former have their own story of arriving on the island and mastering a personal army of Pals, while the latter have a unique code of honor. Quests typical for such games were absent. The lore is explained through scattered notes and item descriptions throughout the world. A full-fledged storyline is planned for the final version by the developers.

The world is open from the start, but movement is restricted by the level difference between the protagonist and each zone. The higher the level, the greater the chance of dying from a sneeze from the natives, who deal a lot of damage and have high health. Throughout the map, there are fast travel points, a teleport to your base, and a choice of respawn points after death. Over time, flying on Pals becomes available, similar to "Zelda." The map is interactive, serving as both a navigation tool and a hint, visualizing the habitats of Pals, treasures, bosses, etc. Aside from nature, the world's content is limited — a few raider bases, some towers, and dungeons.
<div class="article_block a_text">

In Palworld, there are more than one hundred Pals, each with its own characteristics, strengths and weaknesses, a set of abilities, and, most importantly, an element (water, fire, earth, lightning, and others, a total of nine). The interaction between elements resembles "rock-paper-scissors," where some elements are stronger than others but weaker than the third. For example, fire Pal attacks will deal more damage to ice Pal than vice versa, and will be weaker against water Pal attacks.

</div>
<div class="article_block a_text">

Characteristics (attack, defense, endurance, health, carry weight, work speed, and food consumption) define the individuality of each Pal and improve as they level up. This includes training and battles, completing quests that affect experience gain, using useful equipment, and even weapons.

</div>
<div class="article_block a_text">

In early access, Pals had their own skill system:

</div>
<div class="article_block a_list">
<ul>
 	<li>Active — used in battle, up to three can be learned, each further enhanced by the element's attributes;</li>
 	<li>Passive — unique to each Pal, randomly determined upon its appearance. They can positively (or negatively) affect the assistant's build, allowing it to deal more damage but perform poorly, or vice versa;</li>
 	<li>Partner — activated when a Pal is in the player's squad, affecting the hero. For example, they can increase carry weight or running speed. Unique to each type.</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="article_block a_text">

Pals are not just companions and fighters. They are reliable helpers in household tasks, with a predisposition for certain jobs: resource gathering, caring for plantations, carrying loads, and so on. Developing a base, where the automation of processes is built on the labor of Pals, adds a unique economic strategy to the gameplay. But they also require care: timely feeding, washing, letting them sleep, and monitoring their mood. A little monster that catches "depression" can cause chaos.

</div>
<div class="article_block a_text">

There is a breeding mechanic for Pals aimed at obtaining only the desired skills and characteristics. By choosing a pair with suitable traits, feeding them sweets, and placing them in a cozy corner, after some time, the player receives an egg, which is then raised in the Incubator. The result of breeding is unpredictable, but it "inherits" from the chosen Pals. After trying a couple of times, the desired combination will appear. During the breeding process, there is a chance to obtain a completely new species.
<div class="article_block a_text">

In early access, Palworld offered a poorly tuned but standard "entertain yourself" attraction: explore the world, collect pals, unlock new technologies, and develop, set up new bases and workbenches, defend against enemies. The patterns, despite the initial variety, quickly become predictable, reducing gameplay to repeating similar actions.

</div>
<div class="article_block a_text">

All activities grant experience, which accumulates into levels. As it grows, the character earns technology points, which are used to purchase blueprints for crafting new tools, weapons, various devices, or structures, as well as attribute points. By distributing the latter among 5 attributes — health, damage, stamina, carry weight, and work speed — the player eases their survival. No auto-leveling — neither for the world, nor for the protagonist, nor for the Pals.

</div>
<div class="article_block a_text">

Starting with wooden clubs and stone axes, in 20-30 hours the player progresses to crossbows, rifles, and even rocket launchers. To create weapons, it is necessary to gather the required materials, but crafting in early access was simple. There is an inventory where the protagonist keeps the loot collected during the game, distributing weapons and first aid items across slots. Excess is stored in chests.

</div>
<div class="article_block a_text">

The main goal for completing Palworld in early access was to defeat the leader of each of the five game factions. To do this, it was necessary to invade the namesake Tower, defended by its faction, and battle with the personal Pal and the boss itself. On the way to the goal, factions could pursue the player for violating their code of conduct and also invade their bases. Among such violations are attacks on ordinary people, faction members, or active extermination of Pals.
<div class="article_block a_text">

Palworld can be played with 2-4 friends, either by setting up your own game server or on dedicated servers, where up to 32 players can run around. Multiplayer requires creating a new character, which will remain linked to the world or server where it was created. That is, on each new server or in cooperative mode, there will be a separate character with its own progression.

</div>
<div class="article_block a_text">

In addition to joint actions for upgrading the base and gathering resources, in both modes, players can exchange created Pals, trade items, and join existing guilds. PvP in multiplayer was added later and under its own rules. As well as transferring characters between different servers and platforms.
